AquaWatch Water Risk Ranking

Ranking water risk for every municipality in Ceará, Brazil, and showing how far to trust each score

  • Python
  • GeoPandas
  • Rasterio
  • Zonal Statistics
  • K-Means
  • Sensitivity Analysis
Living Stones Foundation

Business value

All 184 municipalities ranked into 55 Critical, 65 High, 40 Medium and 24 Low tiers, with a verification table that tells field teams where to check first.

01 · Business problem

Field teams needed to know which of Ceará's 184 municipalities faced the highest water risk, but 142 of them (77%) had no utility records. A simple ranking would have hidden how uncertain it really was.

02 · My approach

I joined census, utility and satellite data in one reproducible pipeline, normalized risk by percentile rank instead of min-max scaling so outliers like Fortaleza and Jaguaribara could not distort the index, and let K-means find natural tier breakpoints instead of imposing policy thresholds.

03 · What I built

A 13-step Python pipeline over IBGE Census, SNIS utility and JRC satellite data, zonal statistics on 338MB of raster tiles with Rasterio and GeoPandas, a 0.05 to 0.774 risk index, K-means tiers, a 3-dimension confidence framework, worst-case sensitivity analysis, and a verify_priority field for a dispatch-ready verification table.

What changed my thinking

The corrections, tradeoffsand decisions behind it.

  1. 01

    The missing data was the finding

    77% of municipalities had no utility records. Instead of quietly filling the gap, I built a confidence framework, and it flagged none of the 55 Critical ratings as high-confidence.

  2. 02

    Min-max scaling would have misled

    A few extreme municipalities would have squeezed everyone else together, so I normalized every risk variable by percentile rank.

  3. 03

    Tiers came from the data, not a policy line

    K-means (k=4) found natural breakpoints, and a worst-case sensitivity test (0% coverage imputation) checked how stable each tier was before anything reached the field.
